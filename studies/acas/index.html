Overview

Advanced Clean Air Solutions tests for mold and indoor air quality in luxury homes across Santa Barbara, Ventura and North LA.

The service was premium. The website wasn't. A generic outdoor photo, a rigid navy and orange palette, and dense text you had to read just to figure out what the company did. They weren't getting enough qualified leads.

I led the redesign as lead UX/UI designer at Alpha Efficiency, a US design agency. A small UI cleanup wasn't going to fix this, so we rebuilt the visual identity and the page structure.

The Problem

Why the Old Site Fell Short

A generic first image

The hero showed a beach, not air quality work. Nothing said what the company did.

Reading before knowing

Visitors had to get through dense blocks of text just to understand the offer.

A corporate palette

Navy and orange felt dated and corporate, far from the homes they work in.

A demanding form

The contact form was split into two columns and asked a lot of the eye.

Hard to scan

Long sections looked alike, so nothing guided the eye down the page.

A lot at stake

People call them worried about their home and their family's health. They expect clarity from the first second.

Approach

Calm First, Then Structure

My first reaction was that a UI tweak wouldn't be enough. The whole visual identity was working against them. So the plan got simple: show what they do the moment the page loads, and make the site feel like the homes they work in.

We tested a few directions. First the old palette, then brighter blues on white. The one that stuck was a soft gradient, because it felt calm and healthy, which is what you want from the person checking your air.

The copy was written by someone else, so my job was the structure: a new page template that breaks long content into short, scannable sections, starting with the homepage.

  • Show the work

    The hero shows what they actually do: a technician testing a wall.

  • Calm over corporate

    Soft gradients and warm photos that feel like a home, not a brochure.

  • One clear path

    The form went from two columns to one, so the eye only moves down.

Outcome

A Site That Matches the Service

The best moment was the final presentation. The client said it was what they had pictured but couldn't put into words.

The conversion rate went up, and the brand finally looks like the service behind it.

Hindsight

What I Learned

I'd use fewer cards

The copy arrived in a Google Doc with the card content already in tables, so the layout followed that format. It's a content-heavy site, and almost everything ended up in a card. Next time I'd propose lists, short text sections and visuals early.

AI needs a fact-checker

I made all the hero images with AI, working out concepts and prompts with Claude and generating them in Nano Banana Pro. The tools kept getting the air quality equipment wrong. I only caught it by checking against the real thing.

The feeling was the brief

The gradient won because it felt calm and healthy, not because it was prettier. For a service people call when they're worried, how the site feels matters as much as what it says.
