Overview

Kourosh Dini is a psychiatrist, author, musician and podcaster. Each part of that work had grown its own website.

Someone who found him through the podcast had no clear path to his books or his Waves of Focus community. Four sites meant four front doors, and nothing connecting them.

I led the redesign as lead UX/UI designer at Alpha Efficiency, a US design agency.

Approach

The Person First, Then the Map

Before opening Figma, I interviewed Kourosh to understand the person behind the brand. One idea kept coming up: “Mastery and meaningful work develop from guided play.” He's a therapist and a video game enthusiast, and he believes real productivity comes from enjoyment and calm.

That changed the brief. The site couldn't be a directory of services. It had to feel calm and a little playful, so the design itself would make his point.

Then came the structure. I audited the content of all four sites and mapped one new information architecture, so a patient looking for therapy and a creative looking for productivity advice could both find their way.

  • One front door

    A single domain, with one clear menu and a modular homepage that routes people to what they came for.

  • Two clicks away

    The goal was simple: anyone reaches what they need in two clicks, and discovers the rest along the way.

  • Calm and play

    Whitespace, muted colors and custom illustrations, in place of stock photos.

Details

From Four Sites to One

Structure came first, then the layout, then the look.

Outcome

One Home for All of It

Kourosh now has a single home for everything he does. Traffic from the podcast and his speaking events has one clear path to the community and the clinical practice.

The conversion rate went up, and the dead ends of the old sites are gone.

Hindsight

What I Learned

The interview changed the brief

I came in to solve a structure problem. Talking to Kourosh showed me the site also had to feel like his philosophy, and that shaped everything after it.

Gray boxes keep you honest

Without colors or type to look at, the conversation stayed on whether the routing worked for such a mixed audience.

A point of view is easier to design

“Calm and play” gave me something to check every decision against. A list of services wouldn't have.
