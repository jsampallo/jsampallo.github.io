Overview

I built this for a contest inside a bootcamp run by Pareto Talent. The brief was real: design the lead magnet for Pareto's own funnel, from the ad to the sales call.

Pareto finds, trains and places executive assistants from Latin America, which it calls right hands, with US founders doing $500K+ a year. Every sale starts with one conversation, the Matching Call.

It's a fully working lead magnet: live pages, a connected CRM, automations, emails and ads, all designed and built in code with Claude Code. It never ran on paid traffic, so this is a story about the thinking and the build, not about conversion numbers.

The Problem

Cold Traffic With Nowhere to Land

Too soon for a call

Pareto wanted paid ads to founders who had never heard of it. A cold founder isn't ready to book a sales call.

No reason to opt in

Nothing sat between the ad and the sales team, so there was no reason to leave an email.

No filter

Nothing told a qualified founder from a curious one before the team spent time on a call.

Founders don't put off this hire because they doubt they need help. They put it off because they don't know who to hire or what they'd hand over. One Pareto client said it better than I could: “The reason I need them is because I don't have time. And the reason I can't find them is because I don't have time.”

Approach

Map the Whole Journey First

Before deciding what the lead magnet would be, I mapped the path from the ad to the call. That gave every later decision a job to do in the system, not just on the page.

  • One qualifier

    Annual revenue, with the line at $500K. It's the only thing that routes a lead.

  • Two lanes

    Qualified founders go to a calendar. Everyone else keeps the blueprint and gets nurture emails.

  • No dead ends

    Founders who don't book get a nudge, and no-shows get a rebook message.

The Format

A Sample of the Real Product

I broke down the lead magnets of Pareto's three closest competitors. All of them ended at a call, and all of them handed the founder something personal: a level, a report or a number. None of them showed the founder the person they actually need.

That became the opening. The lead magnet is a quiz that builds a spec of the founder's ideal right hand: work style, skills, tools and the first three things to hand off. It's a sample of what Pareto sells, delivered as software.

One honesty rule held for the whole project: the card is a spec, never a person. It has no name, uses an illustrated avatar and never implies that matches have been found.

The Result

I designed the result first and worked backwards. If a question didn't change the output, it was cut.

Outcome

A Full Funnel in Five Days

From the first map to a deployed, tested funnel took five days, and it shipped on deadline: landing page, quiz, CRM-connected form, revenue routing, two result pages, booking, a confirmation page, the follow-up sequence and five ads.

I handed it in with a Loom walkthrough of the whole journey, the creatives and the message list.

It never ran on real traffic, so there are no conversion numbers. If it went live, I'd start with click-through per ad, cost per lead, qualified rate and opt-in to booked rate.

Hindsight

What I Learned

The cuts did the most work

My first draft had six traffic sources, a referral lane, retargeting and a third “not a fit” lane. One qualifier, two lanes and ten questions could ship in days, and the rest can come back once there's data.

A quiz can feel like a test

Nobody clicks an ad hoping to get graded. Every answer stayed the same, but building a person felt like momentum instead of judgment.

The result can't steal the call

An early draft promised the founder their profile again on the call, after the quiz had already given it away. Separating the what from the how gave the call its own reason to exist.
