Overview

Qata gives small food businesses (burger places, pizzerías, bakeries) a digital catálogo. Their customers browse it, build an order, and send it to the owner's WhatsApp, already written.

In small Argentine towns, WhatsApp already is the order system: voice notes, photos of a printed menu and a lot of “¿qué querías?”. The alternative, delivery apps, takes a commission on every sale and keeps the customer.

I designed and built Qata on my own, about an hour a day, with AI (Claude Code). After a seven-month pause I came back to scattered code, audited it, and took it to live subscriptions in ten days.

The Problem

Why Ordering by WhatsApp Breaks

Orders in pieces

An order arrives as five messages and a voice note. The owner rebuilds it by hand, then asks for the address again.

A cut of every sale

Delivery apps tidy up the mess, but they keep a percentage, and the customer.

No app, no account

Nobody downloads an app to order a pizza. The store has to open from a link in an Instagram bio.

A link can't report back

A WhatsApp link opens the chat with the text typed. It can't tell anyone whether the customer actually hit send.

Run from a phone

Owners manage the shop between orders, on their phone, with no technical background.

Slow phones, thin data

Customers in small towns are often on older phones and patchy mobile data.

Approach

Rebuilt in Code, an Hour a Day

Before designing anything, I did an audit. Qata started as a prototype made with another AI tool, and after seven months away the code was spread across files. I mapped every file, every table and every promise the landing made, and marked each one as built or not. It turned up a gap in the database rules that exposed data. I fixed it the same day.

From then on I designed directly in code with Claude Code, and kept a vault of notes as the project's memory: every decision, flow and change written down, so an hour a day was enough to pick up exactly where I'd left off.

  • Nothing new to learn

    WhatsApp stays the channel. Cash and transfers stay the payment. Qata only puts the order in order.

  • Nothing to retype

    Every order arrives complete: products, varieties, time, address, payment and a code.

  • Their brand, not ours

    Customers see the store's name, colors and link. Qata stays out of the way.

Details

For the Customer

One link, no account. The catálogo has to be quick to read and hard to get wrong.

For the Owner

The rush happens at nine on a Friday night, with the fryer on and the phone buzzing. The panel has to keep up without asking for attention.

Setting Up the Store

Owners build the store themselves, on their phone, without taking a single photo if they don't want to.

Outcome

Live, and Ready for Its First Ten

Qata is live at qata.ar. Mercado Pago subscriptions went live on October 28, the terms and privacy policy are written, and the name is filed as a trademark.

Next come ten founding businesses. They use Qata free for as long as it exists, in exchange for real orders and honest feedback. The first real numbers will come from them. The goal is 200 businesses by October 2027.

Hindsight

What I Learned

I audit first now

Coming back to seven-month-old code, my instinct was to redesign. Mapping what actually existed came first, and it found a data leak and promises the product didn't keep yet. Now I check every claim on the landing against the code.

Some things you just can't know

A WhatsApp link can't say whether the message was sent. Instead of guessing, the order waits as “por confirmar” until the owner sees it arrive. One tap per order buys honest numbers.

My smart guess wasn't smart

Photo suggestions picked the wrong burger when two photos shared a word. Showing every match, most specific first, worked better than trying to guess right.
